Max Ellis, PhD researcher in psychoacoustics, University of Salford

Short bio 42 words

Max Ellis is a Defra-funded PhD researcher in psychoacoustics at the University of Salford. He studies how people will perceive future sound environments and Net Zero noise sources (delivery drones, air taxis, heat pumps and electric-vehicle warning sounds) before they arrive, and trains A.I. models to predict the human response.

Full bio 138 words

Max Ellis is a psychoacoustics researcher from Glasgow, based in Manchester, and a Defra-funded PhD candidate at the University of Salford’s Acoustics Innovation Institute. His research recreates future soundscapes, with delivery drones, air taxis, heat pumps and electric-vehicle warning sounds added to real places, and plays them to listening panels. Machine learning models trained on those responses predict who will be annoyed, by how much and why, giving regulators evidence to set standards and engineers a target for quieter machines. He came to acoustics through music, recording and mixing bands before studying music technology, sound for film and artificial intelligence. He teaches at the University of Salford, the University of the West of Scotland and St Mary’s University, London, and is a member of the NASA Unmanned Aircraft Systems Noise Working Group and the Institute of Acoustics Scottish Branch committee.
