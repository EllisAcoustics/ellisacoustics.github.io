Max Ellis BDes MSc AFHEA

PhD Candidate in Psychoacoustics

Acoustics Innovation Institute, University of Salford
EPSRC Centre for Doctoral Training in Sustainable Sound Futures

Frequency scale from 63 Hz to 16 kHz, marking where the noise of the sources I study is concentrated: next-generation wind turbines around 100 Hz, air-source heat pumps around 200 Hz, fixed-wing eVTOL aircraft around 400 Hz, electric cars around 1 kHz, drones around 2.5 kHz and e-scooters around 5 kHz.

Drones, air taxis and heat pumps are about to change how our towns and cities sound forever. I work out how people will perceive them — before they arrive.

Noise already causes more ill health in Europe than any environmental factor except air pollutionWHO, 2011. The next wave of technology will introduce noise that loudness alone cannot accurately judgeHouse of Lords, 2023: the high-pitched whine of a rotor, the tonal hum of a compressor, rumbles of large aircraft flying low over gardens. My PhD research, sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra), seeks to accurately simulate these future soundscapes to gather response data from listening panels, for machine learning models to predict who will be annoyed, to what extent, and why. That gives regulators evidence to set standards on, and engineers a target for quieter machines, before they are everywhere.
