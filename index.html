Psychoacoustic Assessment and Machine Learning-Based Modelling of Novel Noise Source Perception in Complex Soundscapes

ellisacoustics.github.io · linkedin.com/in/psychoacoustic · github.com/ellisacoustics · orcid.org/0009-0007-5909-854X

Frequency scale from 63 Hz to 8 kHz, marking where the noise of the sources I study is concentrated: next-generation wind turbines around 100 Hz, air-source heat pumps around 200 Hz, passenger multicopter eVTOL aircraft around 400 Hz, the warning sounds of electric cars and e-scooters around 1 kHz, and drones around 2.5 kHz.

Net Zero is bringing new technology into our towns and cities – air taxis, electric cars, heat pumps and delivery drones – with sounds we’ve never lived with before. I research the effect these noise sources have on us, before they arrive.

Noise is a “silent killer”, second only to air pollution in its burden on health in EuropeWHO, 2011. The next wave of technology will introduce noise that loudness alone cannot accurately judgeHouse of Lords, 2023: the high-pitched whine of a rotor, the tonal hum of a compressor, rumbles of large aircraft flying low over gardens. My PhD research, sponsored by the Department for Environment, Food and Rural Affairs (Defra), seeks to accurately simulate these future soundscapes to gather response data from listening panels, for machine learning models to predict who will be annoyed, to what extent, and why. That gives regulators evidence to set standards on, and engineers a target for quieter machines, before they are everywhere.

The Gap

At the same sound level, some sources annoy far more people than others. Wind turbines, the first Net Zero source studied at scale, sit well above road, rail and aircraft. Drones, air taxis, heat pumps and e-scooters have no agreed curves yet.

How the Research Works

  1. 01

    Soundscape

    Real places are recorded, then drones, eVTOL aircraft, heat pumps and EVs are added at realistic levels.

  2. 02

    Auralisation

    Each scene is rendered spatially, so listeners hear it as they would standing there.

  3. 03

    Listening Panels

    Participants rate annoyance in controlled jury tests, across different ambiences and contexts.

  4. 04

    Machine Learning

    Models learn which acoustic, operational and contextual factors drive the ratings.

  5. 05

    Prediction

    Annoyance can be estimated for scenarios that do not exist yet, giving regulators evidence and engineers a target.

  6. 06

    Findings

    Models trained on listener ratings predict drone and aircraft annoyance more reliably than traditional metrics alone. See the papers
